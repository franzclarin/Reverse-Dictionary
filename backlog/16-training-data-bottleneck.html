Is training data the bottleneck? A pre-retrain diagnostic

✓ Done
In plain terms

Before spending weeks teaching the model, check whether teaching it is the thing that is wrong.

Today — three tickets (RD-09, RD-14, RD-15) all assume the model needs better training material. They were written before the index changed, and one of their central assumptions has since stopped being true.

Do — two cheap measurements. First, re-check the assumption against the live index. Second, try several ready-made models that anyone can download, on this exact task, and see whether any of them beats the one we trained.

Why it matters — if a free off-the-shelf model wins, the answer to "should we gather more training data" is no, someone already did. If none of them wins, the retrain finally has a real case instead of a hopeful one. Either way it costs an afternoon of local compute and no data collection at all.

Result — done 2026-08-28. The assumption was wrong, and nothing off the shelf beat the model we already have. A model trained on 215 million question–answer pairs — the same size as ours — scored 7 points worse. Nothing shipped; the retraining tickets are answered rather than started.

Gates: RD-09, RD-14, RD-15 — all three resolve on this number Follows: RD-12, whose gate on those tickets has now opened Touches: two new dev-only scripts, eval/. No application code unless the ship gate below is met. Cost: 1h23m of local compute, no API spend, no database writes, no production exposure Write-up: eval/METHODS.md §14

RD-12 came back negative, which opened the gate on the three retraining tickets. That makes the retrain the next thing in the queue — and the first time anyone has had to take its premises seriously rather than file them. Two of them do not survive being checked.

Finding 1 — the register gap is closed (measured 2026-08-28)

RD-14 exists because "the model has only ever seen terse definitions; nobody types gloss text." That was true, large, and measured — on the lemma index. On the index that actually serves users it is noise.

The tripwire slice is 93 paraphrases of dictionary definitions carrying documented meta.leakage: "paraphrase" — maximum register match plus leakage. On the live index it scores below the blind authored slice at rank 1, and its targets are the easier ones (86.0% inside the top 100 against 77.0%). Conditioned on being retrievable at all, the register-matched slice is worse: 27.5% against 31.2%.

RD-02 closed the gap RD-14 was written to close, at zero training cost. Reproduce with npx tsx scripts/probe-register-gap.ts.

Finding 2 — the remaining failures do not have a register shape

From prod_gloss_shipped.json, authored reachable (n=287): rank 1 = 69, ranks 2–10 = 80, ranks 11–100 = 72, never retrieved = 66 (23.0%). The never-retrieved slice has the same frequency profile as the whole set (zipf median 2.93 against 2.99) and is spread across all four authoring styles. What it looks like is a near miss in the right neighbourhood:

A query that is a paraphrase of a definition still reaches rank 1 only ~24% of the time. That is an encoder failing to discriminate finely among 117,791 glosses — not a distribution mismatch that more query-side data would repair.

The fine-tune wins. Every 384-dimensional alternative lost to it, and the one trained specifically on 215M question–answer pairs lost by 7.0 points (35 regressions against 15 wins, p = 0.007) — the only result in the sweep significant in either direction. That arm was chosen as the direct proxy for RD-09: same architecture, same width, same layer count, differing only in what it was trained on. Holding capacity fixed and changing only the training corpus made retrieval worse.

Capacity buys a little, and not enough. The single arm that scored above the control was five times its size at twice the width — and +2.8 points is a null result under the bar pre-registered above, at p = 0.32. It also fails both hard ship conditions: 768 dimensions has nowhere to go in a halfvec(384) column under Neon's ceiling, and its ONNX artifact does not fit RD-11's in-bundle budget. Nothing shipped.

Richer document text does not help either. Adding WordNet's usage examples to the indexed definition cost 1.4 points and raised echo — the competing "the gloss is information-poor" hypothesis, measured rather than argued.

Control cross-validation — the check that had to pass first

full_gloss_ft and prod_gloss_p100 search the same 117,791 synsets with the same model, one by exact scan and one at probes=100. They agree to the digit: lenient R@1 25.4% against 25.4%, strict 21.6% against 21.6%. Two independently built indexes — one in Postgres, one a local file — landing on the same number is what licenses reading the other five cells at all.

Pre-registration — recorded before any encoder number existed

A candidate encoder beating the fine-tune control by fewer than ~6 points of lenient Recall@1 on the authored reachable slice is a null result, not a win — including when the paired McNemar test reaches significance. This is eval/METHODS.md §9a's pre-committed bar, applied unchanged.

Echo is reported beside it as a primary metric: a recall gain that arrives with an echo rise is a trade, not a win.

Six cells are built. The winner is selected on lenient R@1 and nothing else, and a post-hoc maximum of six cells is to be reported as exactly that — the discipline RD-12's fusion control was held to.

Only the control cell is comparable to prod_gloss_p100. Every cell is an exact brute-force scan; production is an approximate index at probes=40. All cross-cell comparisons are exact-against-exact and paired.

Ship gate — three conditions, all required, none of them about recall alone: (1) the delta clears the bar above with p < 0.05 paired and echo not materially worse; (2) the model is 384-dimensional, because GlossEmbedding is halfvec(384) and VocabEmbedding still holds 452MB of Neon's ceiling; (3) its ONNX artifact fits RD-11's in-bundle budget — /api/lookup traces at 151.6MB against a 250MB limit. A 768-dim winner fails (2) and (3) and becomes a follow-up ticket, not a deploy.

Recorded 2026-08-28, before scripts/build-encoder-cell.ts was run for the first time.

Prerequisite knowledge

The lever that has worked here twice is representation, not training

Fine-tuning bought +4.5pp over its own base model on the lemma index — below this project's bar. Changing what was indexed bought +12.9 to +15.7pp with no training at all. An off-the-shelf encoder swap is another representation change, and it is the cheapest one left.

Why an off-the-shelf model is a fair test of "more training data"

multi-qa-MiniLM-L6-cos-v1 is the same architecture and size as the production fine-tune — 6 layers, 384 dimensions — pretrained on 215M question–answer pairs. Holding capacity fixed and changing only the training corpus is precisely the experiment RD-09 proposes, already run, by someone else, for free. If it wins, RD-09's hypothesis is confirmed and its work is largely unnecessary. If it loses, RD-09 is asking for something the architecture may not be able to hold.

The 23% is the only slice this can address

The target is inside the top 100 for 77.0% of queries. That 53-point middle belongs to reranking, and RD-12 has shown no off-the-shelf tool reaches it. Retrieval work — this ticket, RD-09, RD-14 — competes for the 23.0% that is never retrieved at any depth. Do not let a representation win be written up as if it claimed the 53.

Task

  1. Record the register finding reproducibly, not as a number in a document. Done — scripts/probe-register-gap.ts, reading two committed runs, no model and no database.
  2. Build full-scale local cells, not sampled ones. Done — scripts/build-encoder-cell.ts, WordNet → cell, 117,791 synsets, no database and no pool manifest. This is the first cell family in the project whose absolute recall is directly comparable to a production run.
  3. Reuse the single definitions of everything. Done — readSenses, glossTextFor, embedWith, writeIndex. No second copy of anything.
  4. Infer the vector width; do not assume 384. Done — and it mattered: the 768-dim arm is the only one that outscored the control, so a builder that assumed 384 would have quietly excluded the sweep's most informative cell.
  5. Do not touch the harness's encode path. Done — eval.ts, lib/ and app/ are unmodified. Every candidate is mean-pooled and prefix-free.
  6. Include a content arm as well as encoder arms. Done — gloss_examples at full scale, −1.4 points.
  7. Cross-validate the control before reading anything else. Done, and it passed to the digit against prod_gloss_p100.
  8. Record the decision on RD-09/RD-14/RD-15 explicitly. Done — below, and in each ticket.

The decision

Hold all three retraining tickets. Not cancelled — answered, with numbers, on the specific arguments each of them makes.

  • RD-14 — premise falsified. Its reason for existing is the register gap, and the register gap is +1.8pp on the live index, down from +26.1pp on the one it was written against. There is no longer a phrasing penalty to remove.
  • RD-09 — its core bet was tested by proxy and lost. "Same model, better training corpus" is exactly what multi-qa-MiniLM-L6-cos-v1 is, and it scored 7.0 points worse at p = 0.007. That is not proof that no corpus could help; it is evidence that the obvious one, built at a scale this project cannot approach, actively hurts.
  • RD-15 — unchanged and still last. It depends on RD-14's queries, which are now unfunded, and its own motivation was already corrected down to 3.4 points.
  • No production change. The only arm above the control was +2.8 points (a null result), 768-dimensional, and too large to bundle. The ship gate failed on all three of its conditions independently.

What is left pointing anywhere: the 23.0% never retrieved at any depth is now known not to be reachable by register-matched data, by a QA-pretrained encoder of the same size, by a deeper encoder, by a more modern one, by richer document text, or by five times the capacity. The next honest move is RD-10 — a second, independently written test set — because every one of these verdicts rests on 287 queries written blind by one person in one sitting.

Learnings

!

A ticket's premise can expire without the ticket noticing. RD-14 was written against a measured, real, 26-point effect. Eight days later RD-02 removed that effect, and RD-14 went on sitting in the backlog arguing for weeks of data collection to fix something already fixed. Nothing flagged it, because the fix and the ticket were about different-sounding things. When a change lands, re-check the tickets whose case rests on the state it changed — the same lesson RD-12 recorded about its own superseded diagnostic, arriving a second time in the same backlog.

✓

Someone else's training run is the cheapest possible test of your training hypothesis. RD-09 asks for weeks of sourcing to find out whether a better corpus helps. multi-qa-MiniLM-L6-cos-v1 is that experiment already run — identical architecture, identical width, 215M pairs instead of 181k — and downloading it answered the question in seven minutes. Before funding a training run, check whether the ablation you want is already a public checkpoint.

!

QA and web-passage training data is actively harmful on this task, and that is now two independent measurements. RD-12 found MS MARCO cross-encoders ranking glosses by term overlap. This ticket finds a QA-pretrained bi-encoder losing 7 points on the same set. Different architecture, different stage of the pipeline, same cause: those corpora teach question-to-answer-passage relevance, and a reverse dictionary needs description-to-definition paraphrase. The relation is the thing that transfers or fails to — not the model size, and not the architecture.

✓

The fine-tune is better than its own reputation, and the old framing was measuring the wrong thing. CLAUDE.md had recorded it as worth "+4.5pp, a null result" — true, but that compares it to its own base model. Against five modern alternatives at the same width it wins every time, and the nearest competitor is a null result at five times the parameter count. "Weak lever" was a statement about the gain from fine-tuning, and it had been quietly reread as a statement about the model's quality.

!

The pre-committed bar disposed of the tempting result again. all-mpnet-base-v2 came in +2.8 points and it was the one number in the sweep worth wanting. It is p = 0.32 on 50 discordant pairs, and it is the maximum of five candidates picked after seeing all five. The bar was set in August against a register gap that no longer exists — and the second, independent half of its justification, that n≈287 cannot resolve single-digit deltas, is exactly what did the work here.

✓

Hashing the indexed text identifies a cell that metadata cannot describe. Every cell here is stamped variant: "gloss_synset" because that string is what makes the harness expand synsets — so the metadata literally cannot say whether a cell holds definitions or definitions-plus-examples. Recomputing the input hash for each candidate variant recovers it from the artifact instead of the label, and would catch a stale cell that a label never could.

!

A negative sweep is not the same as a closed question, and the difference is worth writing down. Six cells say the representation is not cheaply improvable. They do not say the 23% is unreachable — only that six specific, cheap things do not reach it. That distinction is the one RD-12 had to make about its 53 points, and it is made here for the same reason: so a later reader does not turn "we tried" into "there is nothing there."
