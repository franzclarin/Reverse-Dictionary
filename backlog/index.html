Start here

What this app is. You type a description — "the smell of rain on dry earth" — and it gives you the word: petrichor. A reverse dictionary. There is no keyword matching anywhere in it; it compares the meaning of your description against the meaning of every word it knows.

The problem it had. It handed your own words back to you. Describing rain returned raininess, rainstorm, raindrop — never petrichor. The tickets call this echo, and most of this backlog is either a step toward fixing it or a way of checking whether the fix worked.

The fix that worked. Search the definitions rather than the words. Instead of comparing your description to the bare word petrichor, compare it to "the pleasant smell of rain falling on dry ground" — which is a far fairer comparison, since both are sentences. Built in RD-01, switched on in RD-02 on 2026-08-27: the right word came first for about 1 search in 4, up from 1 in 10.

What was tried next, and did not work. RD-12. Search finds the right answer for about 3 searches in 4 — it just ranks it too low to see, and re-sorting results already found is a much smaller job than finding more of them. So we added a second, slower model to re-sort them. It made results worse, at every setting. The opportunity is still there and still measured; the off-the-shelf tool for it turned out to be trained for a different problem.

What was tried after that, and also did not work. RD-16. The remaining ideas were all "train the model on better data", so before spending weeks on it we checked the assumption and tried five ready-made models on the same job. The assumption had already been fixed by RD-02 without anyone noticing. And none of the five beat the model we have — including one trained on 215 million question-and-answer pairs, which came in seven points worse.

What is next. RD-10 — a second, independently written test set. Every number on this page comes from 287 queries written blind by one person in one sitting. That was enough to decide RD-02, to reject RD-12 and to hold RD-09, and it is now the limiting factor: the cheap improvements are measured and gone, so anything left is an expensive bet, and betting means trusting the ruler more than it has earned.

Reading order, if you want the story rather than the list:

Size vs. impact

Two "do first" tickets have now come back negative. RD-12's 53-point reranking headroom is real but no off-the-shelf reranker reached it; RD-16 then checked the three retraining tickets' premises and found one already fixed by RD-02 and the central one measurably wrong — a same-size encoder trained on 215M QA pairs scored 7 points worse than the fine-tune. RD-09, RD-14 and RD-15 are held rather than cancelled, and the plot no longer shows anything with a measured payoff. That is what makes RD-10 the next move: every remaining bet is expensive, and all of these verdicts rest on 287 queries written blind by one person. Fix the ruler before betting again. RD-01 sits low only because its impact was entirely borrowed from unlocking RD-02, not direct.
